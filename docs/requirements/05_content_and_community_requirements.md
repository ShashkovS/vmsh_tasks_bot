# Content And Community Requirements

## 1. Content hub replacing the Telegram channel
- The application must include a first-class content hub.
- It must replace the jobs currently performed by the Telegram channel:
  - publication of new tasks;
  - publication of hints;
  - publication of solutions;
  - news and announcements;
  - statistics and plus summaries;
  - links to articles, videos, and external resources.

## 2. Tagged archive and discovery
- Content items must support tags.
- Tags must be usable for discovery in the same practical way as in the current channel archive.
- Users must be able to filter content by:
  - lesson;
  - level;
  - content type;
  - publication period;
  - tags.
- Search must be good enough that users can find:
  - tasks for their level;
  - hints for a specific lesson;
  - old solutions;
  - specific announcements;
  - recurring reference materials.

## 3. Content timing and gating
- Content visibility must respect the weekly lifecycle.
- Examples:
  - tasks become visible at lesson publication time;
  - hints become visible on Saturday;
  - solutions become visible on Sunday after the submission window closes;
  - some materials may remain permanently visible afterward in archive.
- The application must prevent accidental early exposure of gated content.

## 4. Structured lesson pages
- Each lesson should have a structured page that groups together:
  - the task set;
  - hints;
  - solutions;
  - announcements specific to the lesson;
  - statistics or summary materials if used.
- This page must reduce the need to jump across unrelated tools.

## 5. Discussion space replacing the Telegram group
- The application must include a moderated discussion area.
- The discussion space must support:
  - general questions about the circle;
  - clarification questions about statements;
  - discussion of ideas at an allowed level;
  - logistics questions;
  - announcements comments if enabled.

## 6. Moderation rules and tooling
- The discussion space must be heavily moderatable.
- Moderation capabilities must include:
  - deleting or hiding messages;
  - editing moderator notes if that model is chosen;
  - muting or restricting users;
  - closing threads;
  - reporting content;
  - pinned rules and pinned discussions.
- Moderators must be able to enforce the current norm:
  - no spam or flood;
  - no public publication of full solutions before allowed time;
  - discussion can exist, but not in a form that breaks the pedagogical process.

## 7. Community roles
- The application must support users who mainly consume publications and discussions rather than submit tasks.
- This is necessary for:
  - parents;
  - inactive students;
  - broader circle audience.
- Such users must be able to:
  - read publications;
  - search the archive;
  - read public discussion;
  - possibly ask logistical questions if permitted.
- Such users must not get teacher or student task-management capabilities by default.

## 8. Linking content to workflow
- The content hub must not be isolated from the task workflow.
- From announcements and lesson publications, users must be able to navigate directly to:
  - the lesson page;
  - a specific task;
  - hint materials;
  - solutions;
  - discussion thread;
  - oral submission instructions if relevant.

## 9. Editorial workflow
- Admins must be able to prepare content ahead of time.
- Editorial workflow should support:
  - drafts;
  - scheduled publish;
  - preview;
  - revision before publish;
  - controlled update after publish.
