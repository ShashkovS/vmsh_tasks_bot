\subsection*{Examples}

\problem

In the quadrilateral $ABCD$, two sides are parallel and the other two are equal. Is $ABCD$ a parallelogram?

\eproblem

\begin{comment}
\problem

Points $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$ lie on the plane. It is known that $\angle ABC = \angle ADC$ and $\angle BAD = \angle BCD$. Must these points be the vertices of the parallelogram?

\eproblem

\end{comment}

\problem

The diagonals of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ are equal and perpendicular. Must $ABCD$ be a square?

\eproblem

\subsection*{Basic properties}

\problem

Prove that the angle between the altitudes of a parallelogram drawn from the same vertex is equal to one of the angles of the parallelogram.

\eproblem

\problem

The sides of a parallelogram are equal to $5$ and $3$ cm. The bisectors of two angles adjacent to the longer side divide the opposite side into three segments. Find the lengths of these segments.

\eproblem

\subsection*{Double-median}

\problem

In the triangle $ABC$, the median $AM$ is extended beyond the point $M$ to the point $D$ such that $AM = MD$. Prove that $ABDC$ is a parallelogram.

\eproblem

\problem

On the median $BM$ of the triangle $ABC$ the point $E$ is taken. The line $AE$ intersects the side $BC$ at point $K$ and makes equal angles with lines $BM$ and $BC$. Find $BC$ if $BE = 5$ and $EM = 2$.

\eproblem

\subsection*{Towards the midsegment}

\problem

Through each vertex of the triangle $ABC$ draw the line parallel to the side which is opposite to this vertex. Let the new three lines intersect at the points $A_1$, $B_1$, and $C_1$. Prove that the vertices of the triangle $ABC$ are the midpoints of the sides of the triangle $A_1 B_1 C_1$.

\eproblem

\subsection*{Midsegment}

\problem

On side $AB$ of triangle $ABC$, a point $P$ is chosen so that $AP = 2PB$, and on side $AC$ its midpoint $Q$ is taken. It is known that $CP = 2PQ$. Prove that triangle $ABC$ is right-angled.

\eproblem

\subsection*{Varignon parallelogram}

\problem

Prove that the midpoints of the sides of any quadrilateral are the vertices of a parallelogram.

\eproblem

\problem

The midpoints of the sides of a convex quadrilateral are the vertices of a square. Must the original quadrilateral necessarily be a square?

\eproblem

\problem

The segments connecting the midpoints of the opposite sides of a convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ divide it into four quadrilaterals of equal perimeter. Prove that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

\eproblem
